Immediate Priorities

The Window Valet

What to act on right now, in priority order, based on this week's findings
Prepared for Josh LeClair by John Carlo Caintic · Marketing Strategy & Automation · September 10, 2026

Four things are ready to move on right now, one with a hard deadline, two that are one-decision-away from unblocking real work, and one that's fully unblocked and can just get built. This is the priority order to work them in.

1.SEO & Paid Ads Vendor, Hardest Deadline

Shade Strategy's engagement ends September 21st, 11 days out. They currently handle the website, SEO, and paid ads. Nothing should go unmanaged after that date.
  1. Book both vendor conversations this week, not after Shade Strategy ends. Valve+Meter offers a free, no-commitment marketing assessment, book that first. FatCat doesn't list a named free assessment, reach out directly to ask what their intake looks like.
  2. FatCat Strategies is the leaner, more directly comparable fit, works exclusively with home improvement contractors, window and door installers named as a specialty, positions itself as a partner rather than a vendor.
  3. Valve+Meter is a larger, full-service agency that also names windows and doors specifically, more channels (Local Services Ads, geofencing, direct mail) than likely needed to start, but worth hearing out given the free assessment.
  4. Decide before September 21st, not after, so there's zero gap in SEO/ads coverage.

2.Tag Standardization, One Decision, Not a Build

This has been sitting deferred since late August with nothing broken by the wait, until now: it's the single thing blocking the Partner nurture campaign build.
  1. The Partner campaign reuses the proven Drip/Nurture mechanics, branching content and timing based on whether a partner is tagged as a warm lead. That only works with one consistent tag.
  2. Recommended standard: "Warm Lead," replacing the interim "Drip" tag currently applied by "Partner New Leads Opt-in," and used consistently everywhere partner lead temperature gets set or checked. Kept separate from the general customer-facing "nurture" tag so the two audiences never cross.
  3. The ask: a yes on "Warm Lead," or your own preferred wording. That single answer unblocks scheduling the full Partner campaign build.

3.Mapsly, Fully Unblocked, Ready to Build Now

Independent of the QuoteRite decision entirely. Mapsly already has partner-tagged data flowing through two proven, working workflows, the same pattern just needs extending to sales data.
  1. Build one new workflow: triggered on opportunity create/update, posting address, assigned rep, and stage to Mapsly's API, same proven pattern as the existing Partner-to-Mapsly workflows.
  2. Turn on territory auto-assignment against that data as a pilot, and measure it directly against today's manual assignment to Erica or Justin before rolling it out fully.
  3. Document the actual gain (drive time, appointments per day, fuel) to make a clear go/no-go call on expanding further, route optimization, mobile field capture, heatmaps once real revenue data is flowing.

4.QuoteRite, Waiting on Today's Call

The conference call with QuoteRite is happening now/today. Once it's done, this becomes the single most consequential decision in the whole Customer Journey integration project, everything in that plan currently points at "whichever system replaces MyBlindCo."
  1. Bring back specific answers on: whether a live GHL connection was demonstrated, a real cost/timeline for building one if not, and whether a reference customer or milestone-based payment was offered.
  2. Once the replacement is confirmed, the Customer Journey proposal gets a second pass with real answers instead of placeholders, and the build order turns into an actual dated plan.
Bottom line: start the SEO/ads vendor calls this week regardless of everything else, that's the one with a real clock on it. Tag standardization and the Mapsly build can both move in parallel without waiting on anything. QuoteRite is the only one genuinely waiting on new information.
