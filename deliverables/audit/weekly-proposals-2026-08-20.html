Weekly Proposals for Approval

The Window Valet

Eight weeks, eight decisions, one system built the right way
Prepared for Josh LeClair by John Carlo Caintic · August 20, 2026

This breaks the 8-week roadmap into individual proposals, one per week, so you can review and approve each phase on its own terms rather than signing off on the whole engagement blind. Each one covers the objective, what we'll actually do, what specifically needs your input or approval, and what you get at the end. A summary sign-off table is at the end if you'd rather approve everything in one pass.

WEEK 1Stabilize & Clean GHLIn progress, nearly done
Fix the existing foundation before building anything new on top of it.

What this covers

  • Full workflow audit, fixing the gaps that were causing silent follow-up failures
  • Reactivating and repairing dormant automations found along the way
  • Removing single-person bottlenecks in lead assignment
  • Cleaning up duplicate contacts, deals, and tag names

Needs your input

Sign-off on standardizing tag names across the account (affects reporting categories going forward).
Outcome: every new lead is captured, assigned, followed up with, and doesn't fall through a gap.
WEEK 2Lead & Sales Process
Build one clean, predictable process from new lead through sale, regardless of where the lead came from.

What this covers

  • Map the full journey end to end: New Lead through Sold/Not Sold, Drip/Nurture, and cancellation/rebooking
  • Make sure each stage clearly hands off to the next, with no overlap or gaps
  • Replace hardcoded individual assignments with dynamic, rotating assignment throughout
  • Improve recovery for cancellations and unbooked leads
  • Test the full path with sample leads from every major source (Facebook, Google, phone, website, QR code, partner)

Needs your input

Confirm who belongs in each rotation pool (which reps handle which lead types), since that's a staffing call, not a technical one.
Outcome: the same reliable experience for every customer, no matter which channel brought them in.
WEEK 3Reporting & Accountability
Give you and your team real numbers without anyone having to dig through GHL by hand.

What this covers

  • Dashboards for lead volume and source, contact rate, booking and completion rates, cancellations, and no-shows
  • Sales performance by designer and by lead source, proposals sent, and close rate
  • Follow-up activity (calls, texts, emails) and partner/referral activity
  • Marketing ROI wherever the ad platforms give us the data to calculate it

Needs your input

Which 4-5 numbers matter most to you day to day, so those get built first if we can't stand everything up in one week.
Outcome: open GHL and know what's happening without asking anyone.
WEEK 4MyBlindCo Integration
Get precise about what data belongs in GHL, what belongs in MyBlindCo, and how it should move between them.

What this covers

  • Document what each system currently sends the other, and what each is still capable of that isn't being used
  • Decide which system is the source of truth for each piece of customer data, ending duplicate entry
  • Resolve why the MyBlindCo sync exists as separate per-person versions instead of one shared workflow
  • Identify exactly what GHL still needs back from MyBlindCo (quote status, sold status, order status, measurements, install readiness, completion, service needs)

Needs your input

Any MyBlindCo-side documentation or contact access you can provide speeds this up considerably.
Outcome: a clear, documented architecture, no more guessing which system owns what.
WEEK 5Scheduling ResearchResearch only, no build
Figure out the right way to fix scheduling inefficiency before spending time or money building anything.

What this covers

  • Review how consultations, measures, engineering visits, installs, and service calls currently get scheduled
  • Evaluate Mapsly, native GHL, OptimoRoute, Google Route Optimization, and a custom-built option against drive time, territory, workload, and specialty needs
  • Present a recommended direction with estimated cost, effort, and expected benefit

Needs your input

A comfortable budget range for a paid scheduling tool if one turns out to be the right call, this shapes which options are realistic to recommend.
Outcome: a clear, costed recommendation, not a half-built experiment.
WEEK 6Scheduling Prototype
Build and test the scheduling approach approved in Week 5.

What this covers

  • Customer address goes in, the system weighs existing schedule, drive time, availability, specialty, and job duration
  • Staff get ranked appointment options instead of manually checking multiple calendars

Needs your input

This week depends entirely on Week 5's direction and budget being approved first, flagging that dependency now so there's no surprise if Week 6 shifts based on that decision.
Outcome: a working prototype your team can start using, not just a concept.
WEEK 7Partner System
Turn the Trade Partner program into a fully automated, end-to-end system.

What this covers

  • Partner lead intake, attribution, and communication, fully automated
  • Referral and appointment tracking, plus Mapsly partner mapping
  • Partner performance reporting and identifying inactive partners
  • Automated nurturing for partners where it makes sense

Needs your input

Confirm what "good partner activity" looks like to you, so reporting reflects what actually matters to the relationship, not just raw lead counts.
Outcome: the partner channel runs itself instead of depending on manual follow-up.
WEEK 8AI & Advanced AutomationEvaluation only
Identify and rank the next real productivity opportunities, not chase every AI trend.

What this covers

  • Evaluate AI phone answering, missed-call handling, lead qualification, scheduling, and reactivation
  • Automated review requests, customer status updates, and sales follow-up assistance
  • Rank every opportunity by business impact, difficulty, cost, time saved, and revenue potential

Needs your input

Flag any tools or approaches you're already uncomfortable with (for example, around customer data or phone handling), so we don't spend time evaluating options you'd never approve anyway.
Outcome: a ranked, realistic short list, not a hype list.
