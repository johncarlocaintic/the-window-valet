The most expensive gap in the system isn't generating leads, it's what happens after you win them

Your marketing engine works. Leads come in from Google, Meta, phone, QR codes, and partners, and get worked through to a sale. But once someone becomes a customer, the system goes quiet. That's the gap, and it's the single most valuable thing to fix right now, because it's not costing you leads, it's costing you the highest-margin revenue you already paid to earn.

Why This Is the Highest-Leverage Gap to Close

As a strategist, this is where I'd point every client first: a customer who already bought is dramatically cheaper to sell to again, ask for a referral from, or get a review out of, than a new lead is to acquire through ads. Right now, that entire layer of return on your existing marketing spend is missing. Closing this gap doesn't require a bigger ad budget or a new campaign, it requires making automation you've already paid to build actually run.

The Solution

StepWhat it does
1. Repair, then activateFix the specific errors found in both dormant pipelines (broken assignment references, missing checks, a couple of trigger errors) before switching them on, not after.
2. Sequence it as a growth loop, not just adminOrder updates keep the customer informed; the satisfaction check-in (already built with smart logic that protects your public rating) turns happy customers into reviews; the upsell sequences turn partial buyers into full-home customers; all with no new ad spend.
3. Build a standing health checkA short recurring check (part of the weekly report going forward) confirming every customer-facing automation is published, error-free, and actually firing, not just built. This is what stops a whole revenue system from silently going dark again without anyone noticing for months.

Why This Holds Up Long-Term

  • Nothing new to maintain. This uses automations already built for your business, not a new platform or a fragile custom build.
  • Self-checking by design. The recurring health check means a dormant or broken automation gets caught in days, not discovered by accident months later.
  • Documented as it's fixed. Each repaired automation gets a plain-English note explaining what it does and why, so it stays maintainable even if the person who fixed it isn't the one looking at it next year.
The ask: approval to finish repairing and activate both post-sale pipelines this week. No new spend, no new tools, just turning on revenue you've already built and paid for.
