Executive Summary

This is a rolling audit: each workflow is reviewed directly from its Builder canvas as screenshots come in, and scored against four questions: what's working, what's not, how to optimize it, and what should be simplified, consolidated, or rebuilt. This document is organized to match the account's own folder structure (Lead Pipeline, Sales Pipeline, Lead Sources, My Blind Co, Partner Automations). Inactive/draft workflows spotted in the folder listings are intentionally left out of this review rather than audited as if they were live. See the note at the bottom of each folder section for which ones were skipped and why. It covers 60 workflows sent for review so far, 33 active, 9 inactive (Customer Order Flow Pipeline), 7 misc/legacy (Lead Tracker Workflows folder), 5 inactive (Sales Order Pipeline upsell), and 6 more misc/legacy workflows with a shared broken-reference root cause, and will grow as more are reviewed.

Fix this one first, it's not a design opinion, it's a live error. "(Partners) Write Partner Details to Opportunity" (a draft workflow in the Partner Automations folder) shows a visible red error indicator directly on its "Find Contact" step. Every other finding in this document is a judgment call about design; this one is GHL itself flagging that something is currently misconfigured. Worth checking before anything else here, regardless of the workflow's draft status.
Headline finding: the "Opportunity Not Found" (or "Contact Not Found") branch is broken or inconsistent in 10 of the 14 workflows that include a lookup-then-branch step. Every one of these workflows checks whether a record already exists, and every one of them treats the "Found" path as complete while the "Not Found" path is either a dead end or skips a step the found-path gets. Affected workflows: "2. Attempting Contact," "3. Drip/Nurture," "4. Unqualified," "5. Spam," "3. Proposal Sent," "5. Sold," "2. Appointment Completed," "4. Follow Up," "6. Not sold," and "(Partners) Write Referral Email to Opportunity." The consequences scale with what gets skipped, from a missing tag up to "5. Spam" skipping the DND step (a deliverability/compliance risk) and "5. Sold" skipping the "active client" tag (a closed deal falling outside every post-sale workflow). This is the same structural gap, copy-pasted and re-broken across the large majority of workflows that use this pattern, and remains the single highest-value fix in this audit.

Counter-examples: workflows that avoid the bug

Several workflows sidestep this issue, each a different way, worth learning from: "6. Future Appointments" is symmetrical because neither branch does anything beyond updating the opportunity. "1. Appointment Booked" is symmetrical because it fully duplicates its entire downstream sequence on both sides (a different problem, see its write-up). "Appointment Cancellation Workflow," "After Business Hours," "Auto Missed Call Text-Back," "Exterior Screens Facebook Lead Form," "Google Ad (Exterior) to GHL," "Google Ads (home page) to GHL," and the Partner Automations folder's sync workflows all avoid the issue entirely by not doing a Find-then-branch lookup at all.

Pattern: single-assignee bottleneck (7 workflows now)

"1. New Lead," "2. Attempting Contact," "6. Future Appointments," "4. Follow Up," "Auto Missed Call Text-Back," "Exterior Screens Facebook Lead Form," and "Version 3 Facebook Lead Form" all hardcode "Assign to user: Kelli" with no round-robin, backup assignee, or availability check. By contrast, "1. Appointment Booked" assigns to two people (Erica/Justin), "Partner New Leads Opt-in" assigns to a different person entirely (Renee) for partner-sourced leads, and "Auto Missed Call Text-Back" sends its downstream notifications to "Assigned User" dynamically rather than hardcoding a name. All three are patterns worth extending account-wide once the core assignment step is fixed.

Pattern: a working idea gets copy-pasted instead of parameterized (3 dimensions, 8+ workflows)

The same anti-pattern shows up three separate ways: per marketing source ("Google Ad (Exterior) to GHL," "Google Ads (home page) to GHL," "Exterior Screens Facebook Lead Form," and "Version 3 Facebook Lead Form" are four near-identical intake workflows differing only by which tag they apply); per staff member ("My Blind Co - GHL Erica" sits alongside draft "My Blind Co - GHL" and "My Blind Co - GHL Justin" versions doing the same external sync); and per lifecycle event (the "(Partners) Installation Complete" and "(Partners) Lead Confirmation" pairs share an identical Add-Partner-Contact-to-Workflow → SMS → wait 2 hours → email shape). In every case, a working pattern was rebuilt from scratch per variable instead of being built once and parameterized, which also means any fix or improvement has to be applied N times instead of once.

Good practice worth replicating: attribution-then-shared-workflow

"Phone Number Tracking" and "QR Code and Landing Pages Lead Tracker" both do this correctly: each tags a contact by its specific source (6 tracking numbers / 6 landing pages respectively), then funnels every source into one shared destination workflow via Add to Workflow (into "1. New Lead" and "1. Appointment Booked" respectively) rather than duplicating the downstream sequence per source. This is the exact fix "1. Appointment Booked" and the ad-source intake workflows need. The account already has the right pattern in two places; it just hasn't been applied everywhere yet. Reviewing these two also resolved two earlier open questions: the "None" branches in "1. New Lead" and "1. Appointment Booked" aren't dead code; they're the real, necessary landing spot for every contact who arrives via one of these Add-to-Workflow hand-offs rather than the workflow's own named trigger.

Good practice worth replicating: inline documentation

Five workflows in the Partner Automations folder, "(Partners) Workflow: Send Partner Objects to Mapsly," "(Partners) Workflow: Sync contacts to Mapsly," "(Partners) Map Partner Data to Contact," "(Partners) Write Referral Email to Opportunity," and "Trigger Partner Data Sync", all include a plain-English note box directly on the canvas explaining the trigger, logic, and intended behavior. These are also, not coincidentally, some of the cleanest and best-guarded workflows in the entire audit. Worth adopting this documentation habit account-wide, especially for the more complex workflows like "1. Appointment Booked."

Minor pattern: unfiltered inbound webhooks

Both "Google Ad (Exterior) to GHL" and "Google Ads (home page) to GHL" show "No filters applied" on their Inbound Webhook triggers, meaning either will accept any payload sent to that URL with no validation. Worth adding basic field validation or a shared-secret check.

Folder: 1. Lead Pipeline (New)

The core inbound lead lifecycle, from first form submission through disqualification or spam-flagging.

1. New Lead

Status: PublishedLast updated: Jun 22, 2026Trigger: Form Submitted

What's working well

  • Clean core sequence: Form Submitted → Create Opportunity → Assign to user → Condition. A solid, standard lead-capture skeleton.
  • The follow-up cadence (Welcome Email → wait 30 min → Welcome SMS → wait 1 day → internal notification) is a reasonable multi-channel touch pattern.
  • Internal staff notifications exist so the team knows a lead came in, not just the contact.

What's not working

  • The condition branch looked tautological at first, but this is now resolved (see Executive Summary). "Phone Number Tracking" enrolls contacts here via Add to Workflow, which never fires the "Form Submitted" trigger, so those contacts correctly land in the "None" branch every time. The branch is real and necessary, not dead code.
  • Single point of failure on assignment. "Assign to user: Kelli" is hardcoded with no round-robin or backup.
  • No visible exit condition. Nothing on the canvas removes a contact once they book an appointment or reply.

Recommendations to optimize

  • Swap the single-user assignment for a round-robin/user pool.
  • Confirm or add an explicit exit/goal step so booked leads stop receiving nurture texts.
  • Add a business-hours check before the SMS steps, or confirm "After Business Hours" already covers it.

What I'd simplify, consolidate, or rebuild

The two branches (form-submission path and the Phone-Number-Tracking-fed "None" path) are almost entirely duplicated, the only difference is one extra "Add Tag" step, after which both run the identical six-step sequence. I'd rebuild this as one linear path with the tag applied conditionally, then merged into a single shared sequence, so a cadence change only has to be made once.

2. Attempting Contact

Status: PublishedLast updated: May 28, 2026Trigger: Pipeline Stage Changed OR Call Details (outgoing, busy/no-answer)

What's working well

  • The dual trigger covers both the human-driven and system-driven paths into this stage.
  • "Remove from Workflow" up front avoids two workflows fighting over the same contact.
  • The Find Opportunity → Found/Not Found split is good defensive design.
  • The end-of-sequence "nurture" tag is a deliberate hand-off into "3. Drip/Nurture."

What's not working

  • "Opportunity Not Found" is a dead end, creates the opportunity and ends with no internal notification, no tag, nothing. First occurrence of the headline finding.
  • Same single-assignee bottleneck as "1. New Lead."
  • No contact-facing outreach in a workflow literally called "Attempting Contact", everything shown is internal.

Recommendations to optimize

  • Bring "Opportunity Not Found" up to parity with "Opportunity Found."
  • Add a contact-facing SMS/email touch, not just internal notifications.
  • Confirm exactly which workflow "Remove from Workflow" targets and rename the step to be self-documenting.

What I'd simplify, consolidate, or rebuild

The two "Internal Notification: Email" steps a day apart are the same double-notification pattern seen in "1. New Lead", consolidate to one notification with a built-in reminder mechanism. Extend "Opportunity Not Found" to mirror the found-branch logic rather than maintaining two diverging branches.

3. Drip/Nurture

Status: PublishedLast updated: May 29, 2026Trigger: Contact Tag "nurture" added OR Pipeline Stage Changed to "Drip/Nurture"

What's working well

  • The account's long-term nurture engine, and structurally well built: 11 monthly emails ending in a "Go To" loop-back rather than letting the contact fall off after less than a year.
  • The dual trigger (tag or pipeline stage) covers both entry paths.
  • "Remove from Workflow" up front avoids overlap with the prior workflow.

What's not working

  • "Opportunity Not Found" skips the entire 11-email nurture sequence, the most consequential instance of the headline finding, since a contact tagged "nurture" specifically to receive these emails may never get a single one.
  • No visible exit condition across an 11-month sequence, a converted or closed-out contact could keep receiving emails indefinitely.
  • The "Go To" step's destination and any loop cap aren't confirmed from the canvas alone.

Recommendations to optimize

  • Add an exit/goal condition tied to opportunity stage (Sold, Booked, Not Sold/Disqualified).
  • Confirm the "Go To" target and consider a loop counter or re-qualification checkpoint.

What I'd simplify, consolidate, or rebuild

The 11-email drip itself doesn't need simplifying. What needs rebuilding is the entry logic: "Opportunity Not Found" should feed into the exact same drip as "Opportunity Found," not dead-end separately, strong evidence this opportunity-check block should be a shared, reusable pattern.

4. Unqualified

Status: PublishedLast updated: May 28, 2026Trigger: Pipeline Stage Changed to "Unqualified"

What's working well

  • Simple and single-purpose: tags "disqualified" for downstream segmentation, no unnecessary steps.
  • No internal notification here is reasonable, staff likely don't need pinging every disqualification.

What's not working

  • The "disqualified" tag is applied inconsistently, only on the Found path, not on Not Found. Whether a disqualified contact actually gets tagged depends on whether an opportunity happened to already exist.

Recommendations to optimize

  • Add "Add Tag: disqualified" to the Not Found branch.
  • Business decision worth confirming: should truly unqualified leads ever re-enter nurture later, or is this permanent?

What I'd simplify, consolidate, or rebuild

This is now the third copy of an identical Find-Opportunity pattern with an identical gap, consolidate into one shared "ensure opportunity + apply outcome tag" building block.

5. Spam

Status: PublishedLast updated: May 28, 2026Trigger: Contact Tag "spam" added OR Pipeline Stage Changed to "Spam"

What's working well

  • The dual trigger catches spam flags no matter which way the contact got marked.
  • The Found path does exactly what a spam workflow should: Update opportunity, then Enable/Disable DND.

What's not working

  • The Not Found branch skips DND entirely. A contact tagged "spam" without an existing opportunity keeps receiving texts and emails from every other active workflow. Real deliverability/compliance exposure, not just reporting noise.

Recommendations to optimize

  • Treat this as a top-priority fix. Add the DND step to the Not Found branch immediately.
  • Consider moving DND to fire before the Find/Create split so it can never be skipped.

What I'd simplify, consolidate, or rebuild

Same shared-pattern fix as the rest, apply the outcome action after the Found/Not Found split reconverges, not duplicated inside each branch.

6. Future Appointments

Status: PublishedLast updated: May 28, 2026Trigger: Pipeline Stage Changed to "Future Appointments"

What's working well

  • Both branches end at the same place, so there's no asymmetry bug here.
  • Simple, low-risk, keeps the opportunity record in sync with the pipeline stage.

What's not working

  • Hardcoded "Assign to user: Kelli" again.
  • No reminder or confirmation messaging in a workflow named "Future Appointments", confirmed elsewhere that reminders are actually handled by "1. Appointment Booked," but worth explicitly verifying there's no overlap or gap.

Recommendations to optimize

  • Confirm no overlap with "1. Appointment Booked"'s reminder cascade.
  • Fold into the same round-robin/backup-assignee fix as the rest.

What I'd simplify, consolidate, or rebuild

No structural rebuild needed, one of the cleanest workflows reviewed.

Folder: 2. Sales Pipeline (New)

Post-booking lifecycle: appointment through close, plus cancellations.

1. Appointment Booked

Status: PublishedTrigger: Appointment Status (confirmed)

What's working well

  • Assignment goes to two people (Erica/Justin), better than the single-assignee pattern elsewhere.
  • The "Opportunity Not Found" branch is not a dead end here, the only workflow reviewed where both branches are genuinely symmetrical.
  • The reminder cadence (1 day, 2 hours, 1 hour before) is well designed and confirms appointment reminders are handled, just here, not in "6. Future Appointments."

What's not working

  • This workflow is duplicated eight times over. After Found/Not Found, each side fans into four source branches (Facebook, Google, Phone, None), and both sides run all four. That's 8 parallel columns of a ~13-14 step sequence, differing only by which tag applies at the start.
  • Staff can receive up to 6 internal notifications (email + SMS at three checkpoints) per booked appointment.

Recommendations to optimize

  • Confirm "6. Future Appointments" isn't also sending reminders.
  • Trim internal notifications from 6 touches down to fewer checkpoints or one channel per checkpoint.

What I'd simplify, consolidate, or rebuild

The clearest rebuild case in the audit. Needs: one Find/Create Opportunity step, one 4-way conditional tag-by-source branch, then one shared reminder cascade, not 8 full copies. "Phone Number Tracking" and "QR Code and Landing Pages Lead Tracker" already demonstrate the right pattern (tag by source, funnel into one shared workflow), apply it here.

Folder: 4. My Blind Co
