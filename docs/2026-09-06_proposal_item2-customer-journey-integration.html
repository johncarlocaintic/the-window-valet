Customer Journey & Cross-System Integration

A proposal for connecting GHL, the quoting and operations system, QuickBooks, and Mapsly around one customer journey

Prepared for Josh LeClair by John Carlo Caintic · Marketing Strategy & Automation · September 6, 2026, revised September 7, 2026

This is the mapping and proposal work for Item 2, following a customer from the first lead through the completed sale to the actual financial result. Revised as of September 7th: Josh has confirmed MyBlindCo will not continue as the quoting and operations system. The overall shape of this journey, and the order things should get built in, doesn't change, but MyBlindCo-specific investigation has been removed since it's no longer relevant, and the plan now points at whichever system replaces it once that's confirmed. Nothing here gets built yet either way.

1. The Customer Journey Today

Four systems, four different jobs. Splitting them this way was already the right call; the goal here is connecting them, not merging them.

SystemOwns
GHLFront end of the customer journey: leads, source and attribution, communication, follow-up, sales appointments, salesperson assignment, pipeline, cancellations and no-shows, marketing automation. Stays the CRM and lead-management system.
The quoting and operations systemEverything after the sales appointment: quotes, products, measurements, pricing, orders, jobs, installation scheduling, job status. The installation calendar lives here, separate from GHL's sales-appointment calendar. This role is currently held by MyBlindCo, confirmed as of September 7th to be on its way out; the replacement isn't locked yet, but this role in the journey stays the same regardless of which system fills it.
QuickBooksFinancial accounting: revenue, payments, expenses, the financial result of each job.
MapslyGeographic intelligence, eventually surfacing best ZIP codes, revenue and profit clusters, and partner or marketing opportunities.

Walking a customer through all four: a lead comes in and becomes a GHL contact, tagged by source. A sales appointment gets booked and assigned to a salesperson. Once sold, that customer needs to become a customer in the quoting and operations system with a quote, then an order, moving through production and shipping to a scheduled installation. Once the job is complete, the financial side of it, the invoice, payment, and true cost, lives in QuickBooks. Today, the handoff from GHL into MyBlindCo happens through one custom one-way webhook, but since that system is being replaced, this handoff needs rebuilding against whatever system takes its place rather than repaired as-is. Nothing currently carries order or installation status back into GHL, and nothing currently connects a specific job to its QuickBooks financial result automatically, both of those gaps carry over to whatever the new system turns out to be.

The same six steps, with one example customer instead of abstract stages:

StepWhat actually happensWhere it lives
1. LeadA customer fills out a form on the website asking for a quote. GHL creates a contact for her and tags where she came from, Google, Facebook, referral, or otherwise.GHL
2. Appointment bookedSomeone calls her, books a sales visit, and assigns a salesperson to go measure her windows.GHL
3. SaleThe salesperson visits, she likes the price, and the deal gets marked Sold in GHL. This is the exact handoff point that needs rebuilding, her information has to move into the quoting system automatically from here.GHL into the quoting system

5. InstallationA crew installs the product, the job gets marked complete, and she may get a review request.The quoting system

Six steps, three systems, one customer moving through all of them without anyone retyping her information twice. Step 3 is the one that needs to actually work automatically; steps 4 through 6 need to flow back into GHL so anyone can see where an order stands without opening three different logins.

2. Cross-System Customer Matching

No single ID ties a customer together across all three systems today. GHL has its own contact ID and opportunity ID. The quoting and operations system will have its own contact, quote, and order records, structured around its own IDs, whatever that system turns out to be, this needs confirming directly once it's chosen, not assumed from MyBlindCo's structure since that's now a moot question. QuickBooks' native GHL connector handles its own matching internally, most likely by name or email, standard for that kind of connector, but the exact logic hasn't been confirmed either.

The proposed practical match key for now is email plus phone together, since those are the two fields most likely to exist cleanly across systems regardless of how each one structures its own internal IDs. This should be treated as a working assumption, not a final answer, until the actual replacement system's customer record structure is confirmed directly.

The quoting and operations system: unresolved until the replacement is confirmed. 5. Minimum Viable Integration

Revised September 7th: this section previously recommended fixing the existing MyBlindCo webhook as the first build step. That's no longer the plan, MyBlindCo is being replaced, not repaired. The order of priorities below still holds, GHL to the quoting system first, QuickBooks financials last, but step one now depends on which system replaces MyBlindCo and what connection method it actually supports.

Josh's own instinct on this, which this proposal agrees with: GHL opportunity to quote and order first, QuickBooks financials after that, not all at once. In concrete order, once the replacement system is confirmed:

  1. Build a reliable connection from GHL into the new quoting and operations system first. This replaces the old MyBlindCo webhook entirely rather than repairing it. The exact method depends on what the new system actually supports, a native connector, Zapier, or a custom-built integration, which is exactly what needs confirming before any of this can be scheduled.
  2. Extend that connection to carry order and installation status back into a GHL field. This is the minimum handshake needed for GHL reporting to reflect real order status without ever writing back into the operations system's own calendar or order system, staying fully within the read-only scheduling rule below.
  3. Add the QuickBooks financial layer last, once the GHL-to-operations-system link is proven reliable. QuickBooks' native connector already exists and mainly needs a trustworthy upstream customer or job link to attach to.

6. Reporting Structure Design

This is the schema, not a built dashboard, and it depends on section 4's data map being real rather than proposed. Once the minimum viable integration above is in place, the following fields become reportable in one place instead of scattered across three systems: lead source, salesperson, appointments, quotes, sales, closing percentage, average sale, revenue, gross profit, gross profit percentage, ZIP code, product type, and install status.

7. Scheduling Intelligence, Read-Only Only

Hard constraint: never a two-way calendar or appointment sync between GHL and the installation system, whichever system that is. A previous team built exactly that between GHL and MyBlindCo, and it was shut down after causing double bookings and cancellations that didn't propagate across systems, a cancellation on one side left a stale appointment live on the other. Splitting sales appointments (GHL) from installs (the operations system) into two separate calendars was a deliberate, hard-won fix, not an oversight to correct, and that lesson carries forward regardless of which system ends up handling installs.

Any scheduling intelligence here, Mapsly included, stays read-only: surfacing suggestions across both calendars, geography, drive time, existing appointments, salesperson and installer availability, job duration, workload, never writing back into either one automatically. The technical foundation for this already exists today, the native Mapsly-GHL connector is live and already populating real map data, so this is a reporting and suggestion layer to design on top of what's already connected, not something to build from scratch.

8. Sequencing Mapsly

Mapsly's full rollout, best ZIP codes, revenue and profit clusters, partner and marketing opportunities, waits until the GHL-to-quoting-system data foundation above actually lands, since profit-based clustering only means something once real order and financial data is flowing through. That said, early routing and territory exploration can start now, since the connector is already live: there's an existing plotted route in Mapsly, roughly 27 stops, under a user called "Marketing," and it's worth confirming what that route is actually for before assuming Mapsly's paid capacity is sitting fully idle. It may already be a reusable pattern worth building on rather than starting from nothing.
