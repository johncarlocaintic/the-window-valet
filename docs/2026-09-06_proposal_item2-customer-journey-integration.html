Customer Journey & Cross-System Integration

1. The Customer Journey Today

Four systems, four different jobs. Splitting them this way was already the right call; the goal here is connecting them, not merging them.

SystemOwns
GHLFront end of the customer journey: leads, source and attribution, communication, follow-up, sales appointments, salesperson assignment, pipeline, cancellations and no-shows, marketing automation. Stays the CRM and lead-management system.
QuoteriteEverything after the sales appointment: quotes, products, measurements, pricing, orders, jobs, installation scheduling, job status. The installation calendar lives here, separate from GHL's sales-appointment calendar. MyBlindCo held this role previously; Quoterite is replacing it.
QuickBooksFinancial accounting: revenue, payments, expenses, the financial result of each job.
MapslyGeographic intelligence, eventually surfacing best ZIP codes, revenue and profit clusters, and partner or marketing opportunities.

Walking a customer through all four: a lead comes in and becomes a GHL contact, tagged by source. A sales appointment gets booked and assigned to a salesperson. Once sold, that customer needs to become a customer in Quoterite with a quote, then an order, moving through production and shipping to a scheduled installation. Once the job is complete, the financial side of it, the invoice, payment, and true cost, lives in QuickBooks. The old handoff from GHL into MyBlindCo ran through one custom one-way webhook; that connection is being retired along with MyBlindCo and needs rebuilding against Quoterite instead. Nothing currently carries order or installation status back into GHL, and nothing currently connects a specific job to its QuickBooks financial result automatically, both of those gaps carry over and need solving against Quoterite.

The same six steps, with one example customer instead of abstract stages:

Each step's full description: a customer fills out a form asking for a quote and GHL creates a contact for her, tagged by source (1). Someone calls her, books a sales visit, and assigns a salesperson to measure her windows (2). She likes the price and the deal gets marked Sold in GHL, the exact handoff point that needs rebuilding so her information moves into Quoterite automatically (3). Her order gets placed with the factory and tracked through production and shipping (4). A crew installs the product and the job gets marked complete (5). The invoice and true cost get recorded so the business knows whether the sale was actually profitable (6).

Six steps, three systems, one customer moving through all of them without anyone retyping her information twice. Step 3 is the one that needs to actually work automatically; steps 4 through 6 need to flow back into GHL so anyone can see where an order stands without opening three different logins.

2. Cross-System Customer Matching

No single ID ties a customer together across all three systems today. GHL has its own contact ID and opportunity ID. Quoterite will have its own contact, quote, and order records, structured around its own IDs, this needs confirming directly with Quoterite rather than assumed from MyBlindCo's old structure. QuickBooks' native GHL connector handles its own matching internally, most likely by name or email, standard for that kind of connector, but the exact logic hasn't been confirmed either.

The proposed practical match key for now is email plus phone together, since those are the two fields most likely to exist cleanly across systems regardless of how each one structures its own internal IDs. This should be treated as a working assumption, not a final answer, until Quoterite's actual customer record structure is confirmed directly.

3. What Each Platform Actually Exposes

GHL: well understood already, opportunity fields, tags, pipeline stages, and workflow logic have all been catalogued in detail through the account's own workflow review.

Quoterite: field-level access still needs confirming directly with their team. Public research into Quoterite found no native GHL connector, no public Zapier app, and no public API documentation, see the separate Quoterite Evaluation document. The entity types this role needs to expose are quotes, orders, products, installation scheduling, and job status, at minimum, whether Quoterite can actually deliver a usable API, a webhook-receiving capability, or at minimum a data export is the open question.

QuickBooks: native GHL integration confirmed already connected (Settings, Integrations, QB Connect). This is a configuration task, not something that needs to be built.

Mapsly: native, bi-directional connector for HighLevel confirmed live, and it's already syncing real data today, GHL's partner-tag categories are showing up as populated map layers right now, not sitting unused. Enterprise plan confirmed active, 3 seats, with routing and AI capacity still mostly untouched.

5. Minimum Viable Integration

Quoterite's team confirmed directly (2026-09-08) that the GHL connection doesn't exist yet: it's on their own roadmap, a 2-3 month build done entirely by their internal team, at roughly $1,000 setup plus $150 a month ongoing. That changes step one below from "figure out the method" to "confirm the scope and get on their build schedule," since Window Valet cannot build or maintain this piece itself, it's Quoterite's own proprietary platform.

Your own instinct on this, which this proposal agrees with: GHL opportunity to quote and order first, QuickBooks financials after that, not all at once. In concrete order:

  1. Get Quoterite's GHL connection built and confirmed working first. This replaces the old MyBlindCo webhook entirely rather than repairing it, but it's now a vendor-managed build, not something to configure in-house. Confirm the exact field list and timeline before committing, then track it against their quoted 2-3 months.
  2. Confirm that connection carries order and installation status back into a GHL field. This is the minimum handshake needed for GHL reporting to reflect real order status without ever writing back into Quoterite's own calendar or order system, staying fully within the read-only scheduling rule below. Quoterite has described this shape (status changes, quote values, payments, appointments push to GHL via webhook) but hasn't confirmed each specific field yet.
  3. Add the QuickBooks financial layer last, once the GHL-to-Quoterite link is proven reliable. QuickBooks' native connector already exists and mainly needs a trustworthy upstream customer or job link to attach to.

6. Reporting Structure Design

This is the schema, not a built dashboard, and it depends on section 4's data map being real rather than proposed. Once the minimum viable integration above is in place, the following fields become reportable in one place instead of scattered across three systems: lead source, salesperson, appointments, quotes, sales, closing percentage, average sale, revenue, gross profit, gross profit percentage, ZIP code, product type, and install status.

7. Scheduling Intelligence, Read-Only Only

Hard constraint: never a two-way calendar or appointment sync between GHL and Quoterite. A previous team built exactly that between GHL and MyBlindCo, and it was shut down after causing double bookings and cancellations that didn't propagate across systems, a cancellation on one side left a stale appointment live on the other. Splitting sales appointments (GHL) from installs (Quoterite) into two separate calendars was a deliberate, hard-won fix, not an oversight to correct, and that lesson carries forward to Quoterite too.

Any scheduling intelligence here, Mapsly included, stays read-only: surfacing suggestions across both calendars, geography, drive time, existing appointments, salesperson and installer availability, job duration, workload, never writing back into either one automatically. The technical foundation for this already exists today, the native Mapsly-GHL connector is live and already populating real map data, so this is a reporting and suggestion layer to design on top of what's already connected, not something to build from scratch.

8. Sequencing Mapsly

Mapsly's full rollout, best ZIP codes, revenue and profit clusters, partner and marketing opportunities, waits until the GHL-to-Quoterite data foundation above actually lands, since profit-based clustering only means something once real order and financial data is flowing through. That said, early routing and territory exploration can start now, since the connector is already live: there's an existing plotted route in Mapsly, roughly 27 stops, under a user called "Marketing," and it's worth confirming what that route is actually for before assuming Mapsly's paid capacity is sitting fully idle. It may already be a reusable pattern worth building on rather than starting from nothing.

9. Still Open Before This Moves to Building

  • The specific field list still needs item-by-item confirmation. Quoterite described the shape of what pushes to GHL (status changes, quote values, payments, appointments) but hasn't confirmed each field individually, quote amount and status, deposit received, order submitted, production status, products received, installation date and status, installation completed, balance collected, and service call or remake created.
  • Who owns the integration once it's built. Quoterite's team confirmed they retain ownership of the integration and its code, since it runs on their own platform. Worth a direct answer on what happens to that configuration if Window Valet ever needs to switch systems again.
  • No reference customer has been offered for a GHL, or any outside-CRM, integration specifically. Worth asking for one running their HubSpot or Zoho connection instead, since those are complete today.
  • Testing one real Window Valet job end to end across both systems, GHL appointment through quoting, deposit, production, installation, and payment, can't happen until Quoterite's GHL build is actually done, not before.
  • What the "Marketing" user's plotted 27-stop route in Mapsly is actually for, real use or a leftover sample, before assuming anything about how much of Mapsly's paid capacity is genuinely unused.
