2. Anomalies

Sitting in Draft when it should be live: "My Blind Co - GHL Justin" has 308 past enrollments, so it clearly ran for a while before someone reverted it to Draft. Its counterpart, "My Blind Co - GHL Erica," is still Published, and opening both shows they're structurally near-identical: same four booking-source tags, same dedup check, same MyBlindCo webhook, same field update. Since "1. Appointment Booked" assigns leads to either Erica or Justin by name, these look like parallel, salesperson-specific copies of the same sync logic rather than genuinely different automations. That changes the fix here from a simple republish to a real decision for Josh: republish Justin's copy as-is and keep maintaining two nearly identical workflows, or consolidate into one shared MyBlindCo sync that isn't tied to a specific salesperson. Two more Draft workflows still have real, currently-active contacts inside them right now, which is even stronger evidence something here isn't behaving like a retired workflow should: "Appointment Booked Workflow" has 19 people active in it, and "Drip Campaign Workflow" has 2. Before either gets touched, those contacts need to be accounted for, either they finish naturally, or someone moves them, or there's a reason this old version is still doing something the new one isn't. Two more Draft workflows are worth a decision from Josh for the same reason: "(Partners) Write Partner Details to Opportunity" (24 past enrollments, sitting in the current Partner Automations folder) and "05. Measured/Pending Service Workflow" (41 past enrollments, no live counterpart found anywhere else in the account under that name).

Untouched for months: six live workflows are still enrolling people but haven't been opened or touched in months. Trigger Partner Data Sync (185 days), Map Partner Data to Contact (184 days), and all four Partner Communications workflows (102 days each). None of them are confirmed broken, but none have been checked either, and that's exactly the kind of thing that sits quietly until it isn't quiet anymore.

Found along the way, not something the roadmap named up front: GHL's own "Needs Review" list flags two workflows with unresolved errors from the past 30 days, and both are ones this audit already leaned on as evidence that things were working. "My Blind Co - GHL Erica" (the live webhook to MyBlindCo) has failed webhook calls for at least two contacts, Brett Driscoll (Aug 28) and Nicholas Carey (Aug 24). It's live, but it isn't reliably getting data to MyBlindCo. "(Partners) Workflow: Send Partner Objects to Mapsly" failed to send three partner records on August 12th: Fischer Homes - Hampton Walk, Fischers Homes - The Townes at Hampton Walk, and Lennar - Ravinia. Neither error has been marked as read or looked into yet.

Also found along the way: the customer-journey mapping plan assumes GHL already has a live pipeline tracking order and installation status (stages like Production Complete, Order Shipped, Installation Scheduled). That pipeline exists, but every one of its nine workflows is sitting in Draft, inside the inactive folder. Right now, nothing on the GHL side is actually tracking order or install status.

Still open, not yet resolved: the missing existing-customer exclusion the roadmap flagged hasn't been found yet. "New Lead" looked like the obvious candidate and was opened specifically to check; it turns out to already handle that correctly, so it's ruled out rather than confirmed. The two mismatched appointment reminder emails turned out to have a likely explanation once "1. Appointment Booked" was opened: its own builder left a note saying the reminder sequence is repeated four separate times, once per lead source, instead of one shared sequence. Four near-identical copies drifting apart from each other is a simpler explanation than two different workflows disagreeing, though it still needs the four copies compared directly to confirm.

Found while documenting the workflows themselves: "7. Not Booked Workflow" lives in the current "2. Sales Pipeline (New)" folder and is Published, but its own trigger watches stage changes on a pipeline called "Lead Flow Pipeline," not "1. Lead Pipeline (NEW)" or "2. Sales Pipeline (NEW)." That name matches the legacy workflow folder full of Draft duplicates documented in Sort By Function. Either this live workflow was never updated when the account was rebuilt onto the new pipelines, or "Lead Flow Pipeline" is still a real, separate pipeline object quietly running in parallel. What's now confirmed: both "Exterior Screens Facebook Lead Form" and "Version 3 Facebook Lead Form" explicitly enroll contacts into "7. Not Booked Workflow" directly through an Add to Workflow action, bypassing its trigger entirely, so this workflow is almost certainly still receiving contacts through that direct path even if its own trigger never fires. Still worth confirming with Josh whether "Lead Flow Pipeline" itself is dead weight or something still in use, especially since "(Partners) Write Partner Details to Opportunity" also triggers off the same pipeline, so this isn't a one-off reference.

1. Lead Pipeline (New)

1. New Lead Published

New Lead fires when someone submits either the Strategy-Hero Lead Form or the Strategy-Contact Us Form. The first thing it does is check whether the person is already tagged as an existing customer. If so, it stops right there and does nothing further, which is worth calling out because this was the leading suspect for the roadmap's missing-exclusion problem, and it turns out to already handle that correctly. Everyone else gets an opportunity created in the new Lead Pipeline, gets assigned to Renee, and gets tagged and welcomed by email while Kelli Martin, Main Office, and Renee Isley get an internal text. If half an hour goes by and the lead hasn't moved off the New Lead stage, Josh gets a personal text at his own number warning that the lead has gone untouched for an hour; ten minutes after that, if it's still stuck, the lead just gets tagged as escalated with no further outreach visible from there. Two smaller things worth a look while someone's in there: a branch partway through the workflow appears to do the same thing regardless of which way it goes, and duplicate opportunities are allowed on the same contact, which could in theory double up if someone submits the form twice.

1.1 New Lead Follow-Up Task Chain Published

This one runs alongside New Lead, but it's triggered differently: it fires whenever a task assigned to either Erica Stephenson or Renee Isley gets marked complete. From there it just keeps checking: is this contact now sitting in Appointment Booked or Sold? If yes, it's done. If not, it creates another task for Renee asking her to reach out within ten minutes, then checks again, and again, with at least five rounds of this visible in what was captured. It's a solid, deliberately-built safety net, which is exactly why it's odd that it shows zero enrollments ever. Either tasks in this account rarely get completed by Erica or Renee specifically, or this workflow has simply never had the chance to run. Worth asking Josh which it is.

2. Attempting Contact Published

Attempting Contact picks up in one of two ways: either the opportunity's stage actually changes to Attempting Contact, or an outgoing call comes back busy, voicemail, or no-answer. Either way, the contact gets pulled out of whatever workflow it was in before, assigned to Renee, and matched to an opportunity, updating one if it exists or creating one if not. From there, Main Office, Renee, and Kelli all get an email, then another a day later, and after that second day the contact gets tagged "nurture," which is what hands it off to Drip/Nurture below. There's a sticky note on the canvas explaining more of the reasoning here, but too much of it was cropped out of what could be captured to quote directly. This one's clearly in active use: 147 people have passed through it, two are in it right now.

5. Spam Published

Spam fires either when a contact gets tagged "spam" directly, or when its opportunity's pipeline stage changes to Spam on its own. Either way, the contact comes out of whatever workflow it was in, and the opportunity gets found or created and set to the Spam stage with a status of Abandoned, using the same efficient shared-sequence design as Drip/Nurture, where the "just created" path jumps back into the same next step rather than repeating it. From there it moves into an Enable/Disable DND step, though which direction that toggle actually goes wasn't visible in what was captured. Worth a follow-up look, since that's the difference between correctly shutting off marketing to a spam contact and accidentally leaving it on. Nobody gets notified anywhere in this one; it's a quiet cleanup workflow, unlike the others documented so far.

6. Future Appointments Published

Future Appointments fires when an opportunity's pipeline stage changes to "Future Appointment" within Lead Pipeline (NEW). It assigns the contact to Renee, pulls it out of whatever workflow it was in before, then finds or creates the opportunity and sets it to the Future Appointment stage with an Open status, the same duplicate-opportunities-allowed pattern seen elsewhere in this account. What happens after that isn't confirmed. The canvas continues past the point captured, so who gets notified and how someone exits this workflow are both still open questions.

7. Customer Reply Response & Escalation Published

This one fires when a customer replies specifically over SMS. It waits 15 minutes, creates a follow-up task, waits another 15 minutes, and if nothing's changed by then, texts Kelli Martin, Main Office, and Renee Isley: "ESCALATION: [Contact] replied 30 minutes ago and still hasn't been followed up on. Please check Conversations and respond now." What the task itself says, and what happens after the escalation text goes out, isn't confirmed. The canvas continues past the point captured.

7.1 Reply Task Completed, Exit Published

This one fires when a task gets marked complete, but only if the task's assigned user is not Renee Isley. It removes the contact from whatever workflow it was in, then marks something as read. The canvas continues past that point; what's captured stops there. It reads as the exit companion to "7. Customer Reply Response & Escalation" above: when someone other than Renee completes the follow-up task that workflow creates, this pulls the contact out of the escalation chain and clears the notification, so the 30-minute escalation text doesn't keep firing once a reply has actually been handled.

3. Drip/Nurture Published

Drip/Nurture is the workhorse of the account: 810 contacts have gone through it and 717 are in it today. It kicks off the moment a contact gets tagged "nurture," though if that contact already looks booked, it skips the whole thing immediately. Otherwise it settles into a simple rhythm: send an email, wait thirty days, check whether the contact has booked an appointment yet, and if not, send the next one. That repeats for eleven emails in a row, almost a full year of content, and the only way out early is booking an appointment. It's also a cleaner build than New Lead: instead of duplicating all eleven rounds for both the "opportunity already existed" and "opportunity just got created" paths, it uses a single jump step to merge them back into one shared sequence. The one thing that couldn't be confirmed is what happens after email eleven; the trail ends at another jump step whose destination wasn't visible in what was captured. Since this is the exact pattern the Week 7-8 plan wants to reuse for partner nurturing, it's worth getting a clean look at that last step before building the copy.

2. Sales Pipeline (New)

1. Appointment Booked Published

This one comes with its own sticky note left on the canvas by whoever built it, so this description is theirs, not a reverse-engineered guess. It runs automatically whenever an appointment's status is confirmed. From there: it assigns the booked lead to Erica or Justin; checks whether the contact already has a deal on file, updating it if so or creating one if not, with the rest of the workflow running the same either way; checks where the lead came from (Facebook, Google, Phone Call Lead, or another source) and tags it accordingly; sends a booking confirmation email right away; and sends reminders at three points before the appointment (1 day before, 2 hours before, and 1 hour before), each paired with an internal alert to staff. The builder's own note explains why: every appointment needs a confirmation and a reminder sequence regardless of where the lead came from, the source only changes which tag gets applied, not the reminder steps themselves.

The builder also left a warning for whoever edits this next: the entire reminder sequence is currently repeated four separate times, once per lead source, instead of one shared sequence used by all four. That is directly relevant to the still-open "two mismatched reminder emails" item from Anomalies: with four near-identical copies of the same reminder sequence sitting inside one workflow, it would only take one of those four drifting out of sync with the others for two reminder emails to disagree on what kind of appointment is happening, without needing a second workflow at all. Worth comparing the four copies against each other directly before looking anywhere else.
