Quoterite Evaluation

A look at Quoterite as a possible replacement for MyBlindCo, and what still needs answering before deciding

Prepared for Josh LeClair by John Carlo Caintic · Marketing Strategy & Automation · September 5, 2026

This covers what Quoterite actually is, how it compares to MyBlindCo, what would need to happen to connect it to GHL, and the specific questions worth settling on the demo call with their owner. Nothing here is a recommendation to switch yet, that decision depends on answers only the demo can provide.

1. What Quoterite Actually Is

An AI-powered quoting and order platform built specifically for the window covering industry, blinds, curtains, drapery, awnings, not a general-purpose CRM or quoting tool bolted onto this industry as an afterthought. It covers the same ground MyBlindCo does today: quote generation, client management, job tracking, inventory, invoicing, and payments, all in one place. Its own AI features, live in the product today rather than promised for later, include voice-powered search across jobs, automatic meeting transcription and summaries, AI-drafted client emails, an AI quote checker, and AI-assisted price-list entry.

2. How It Compares to MyBlindCo Directly

Quoterite publishes its own comparison against MyBlindCo, and a few concrete facts are worth knowing. MyBlindCo's own accounting connection is limited to QuickBooks Online or Desktop only, no Xero or MYOB support. MyBlindCo's payment processing runs through its own "MyBlindCo Pay" via Stax, at a 2.6 to 3.5 percent transaction fee. Quoterite, by comparison, connects to QuickBooks, Xero, and MYOB as standard, and says it can link to any accounting system on request. Quoterite's own headline claim against competitors generally, including MyBlindCo, is working AI features today versus none, and a stated commitment that customer data stays exportable at any time rather than locked in.

3. Pricing

The tier that fits a retail and installation business this size is Advanced Retail Solutions, priced in the US at $195 a month plus $110 a month for each additional user beyond the first. A setup fee applies but the exact US amount wasn't confirmed publicly, worth asking directly on the demo call.

4. Connecting It to GHL, the Honest Picture

Confirmed directly by Quoterite: the GHL connection does not exist yet. Their CEO, Aaron LeCornu, confirmed it's on their own roadmap, a build done entirely by their internal team, expected in 2 to 3 months. They already have complete two-way integrations with HubSpot and Zoho, just not GHL yet. There is no open API and never will be by design, Quoterite builds, hosts, and maintains every integration themselves on their own proprietary platform, so this is not something Window Valet's own team could set up independently even once it exists. Cost, once built: approximately $1,000 one-time setup plus $150 a month ongoing, with no separate usage limits. One important detail: Quoterite retains ownership of the integration and its code once built, since it runs on their platform, not Window Valet's.

This matters because it's the same shape of problem MyBlindCo already causes, being boxed into whatever a vendor is willing to build, and now confirmed to be true of Quoterite too, just with a real price and timeline attached instead of an unknown one. Quoterite's accounting integrations run in parallel to, not in conflict with, the QuickBooks-GHL connection already being set up separately.

5. What's Still Unverified

  • No free trial, demo account, or sandbox was found anywhere on their site, and Quoterite's reply confirmed there isn't a self-service one, they scope requirements and test internally before deployment rather than handing over a test environment directly.
  • Independent reviews are essentially nonexistent. One listing shows a rating with zero actual written reviews behind it, and no meaningful discussion was found anywhere else online. No reference customer running a GHL, or even another outside-CRM, integration was offered either, worth specifically asking for one running their HubSpot or Zoho connection instead, since those are complete today.
  • The specific field list Quoterite would push to GHL hasn't been confirmed item by item, quote amount and status, deposit received, order submitted, production status, products received, installation date and status, installation completed, balance collected, and service call or remake created. Only described generally as "status changes, quote values, payments, appointments" so far.
  • What happens to the integration and its configuration if Window Valet ever needs to leave Quoterite, since they retain ownership of it, not documented anywhere yet.

6. What Quoterite Confirmed, and What Still Needs Settling

Quoterite's CEO answered most of the original questions directly and quickly, a genuinely positive sign on responsiveness. What's now confirmed: no GHL connector exists yet, it's a 2 to 3 month build done entirely by their own team, at roughly $1,000 setup plus $150 a month, with no separate API usage limits, error logging and automatic retry on failed transactions, and duplicate prevention via unique system IDs. What still needs settling: the exact field list above, a straight answer on what happens to the integration if the relationship ever ends, and a reference customer, ideally on HubSpot or Zoho since GHL isn't built yet. A live demonstration of an actual GHL sync isn't possible until their build is done, so testing one real Window Valet job end to end, from the original GHL appointment through quoting, deposit, production, installation, and payment, has to wait for that as well.

7. Where This Leaves Things

Quoterite looks like a genuine, purpose-built alternative to MyBlindCo on the quoting and operations side, with a real edge on AI features, a stronger stated commitment to data portability, and now a specific, reasonable price for the GHL work. What's changed is the timeline: this isn't a decision that can be tested and confirmed working before committing, it's a 2 to 3 month bet on a vendor's own roadmap, for a connector no other customer is using yet. That's not a reason to rule Quoterite out, but it is the reason to get the ownership question and the exact field list answered in writing before committing to that timeline, not after.
